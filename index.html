Your quit plan

evidence-informed strategies
0Preparation — set yourself up to win
  • Remove every device. Throw out vapes, pods, and chargers tonight. Distance is your strongest ally — a craving that requires a trip to the store is far easier to outlast than one within arm's reach.
  • Map your triggers. Most vaping is cued: after meals, with coffee, driving, stress, drinking, boredom, certain friends. Write your top five down — you'll plan a counter-move for each below.
  • Tell your people. Accountability roughly doubles success odds. Tell at least two people your quit date and ask them to check in on days 3 and 7.
  • Stock replacements. Sugar-free gum, toothpicks, cinnamon sticks, sparkling water, fidget toy. Hand-to-mouth habit needs a stand-in for a few weeks.
  • Consider NRT. Nicotine patches, gum, or lozenges can ease withdrawal, especially for heavy use. Talk to a pharmacist or doctor about what fits you.
1First 72 hours — the storm
  • What to expect: irritability, restlessness, brain fog, intense cravings. This is nicotine leaving your system — it peaks around day 2–3 and then genuinely fades. It's withdrawal, not who you are.
  • The 5-minute rule: cravings crest and pass in 3–5 minutes whether or not you vape. When one hits, smash the panic button below and ride it out — you only have to outlast it, not defeat it.
  • 4 Ds: Delay, Deep breathe, Drink water, Do something else. Pick one instantly when a craving lands.
  • Change your loops. Take a different route, sit in a different chair, swap coffee for tea for 3 days. Breaking the cue chain breaks half the craving.
  • Sleep & sugar: blood sugar dips mimic cravings. Eat regular meals, keep fruit handy, go to bed early — your brain is rewiring and it's tiring work.
2First week — building momentum
  • Move daily. Even a 10-minute walk measurably cuts craving intensity and lifts mood. Exercise is the closest thing to a legal craving-killer.
  • Hydrate aggressively. Water helps flush nicotine metabolites and gives your hands and mouth something to do.
  • Audit your wins. Check your milestones here every morning. People who track progress are significantly more likely to stay quit.
  • Dodge the danger zones. If parties or certain friends mean vaping, it's okay to skip them for one week. It's a week, not forever.
  • Beware "just one." One puff reliably reignites the receptor cycle. The craving for "just one" is the addiction negotiating — name it and decline.
3First month — rewiring
  • Stack a new habit on each trigger. After-meal vape → after-meal walk or tea ritual. Same cue, new reward — that's how habits actually get replaced.
  • Spend the savings visibly. Move your saved money (tracked above) into a separate jar or account weekly and buy yourself something real at day 30.
  • Expect ambush cravings. Random strong cravings in week 3–4 are normal echoes, not failure. They pass faster each time.
  • Mind your mood. If low mood or anxiety persists past a couple of weeks, talk to a doctor — support exists and using it is strength.
∞Long-term — staying free
  • Identity over willpower. Shift from "I'm trying to quit" to "I don't vape." Identity-based change is the most durable kind.
  • Have a stress plan. Most long-term relapses happen during high stress. Decide now what you'll do instead: call someone, walk, breathe, gym.
  • Alcohol is the #1 relapse trigger. For the first few months, decide your response to "want a hit?" before the drink, not during it.
  • If you slip, contain it. A lapse is an event, not a verdict. Reset the timer, learn the trigger, keep your history below — every previous streak still rewired your brain.
  • Help someone else quit. Nothing cements your own freedom like guiding a friend through theirs.
